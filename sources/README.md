## Full-day counting review — 16 November 2026

This review corrects the counting described in the 14–15 November examples above; those examples remain a historical record, not the current expected dates. Reopened the official consolidated [Lei n.º 75/2013](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/legislacao-consolidada/lei/2013-56366098), including arts. 9, 11, 12, 49, 53 and 137; the [Labour Code holiday provisions, arts. 234–235](https://diariodarepublica.pt/dr/legislacao-consolidada/lei/2009-34546475-46746675); and the [IRN confirmation of Lisbon’s 13 June holiday](https://irn.justica.gov.pt/Contactos-do-Registo/Registo-Civil-de-Lisboa). The Junta’s regimento page was fetched again on 16 November and still links `Regimento_Dez2021_pdf.pdf`. A fresh PDF download has the same SHA-256 as the snapshot above. Searches did not identify a later published regimento. Rechecked arts. 24, 25, 33, 35 and 37, including the existing national/local conflict for individual requests. The cover and approval/commencement dates are unchanged.

The [CCDR-LVT legal opinion 1/2022](https://www.ccdr-lvt.pt/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Parecer-Juridico-1-2022.pdf) interprets art. 53(2) as requiring two complete working days between delivery and the session: its Wednesday meeting example requires delivery by the preceding Friday. The former calculation returned the second preceding working day itself and left only one complete working day between. Document delivery now falls on the preceding working day; publicity uses the same full-working-day counting for its two-day advance notice. Neither endpoint counts. Holidays, weekends and the Carnival setting remain excluded.

For convocation, art. 137 establishes continuous/calendar days. The [TCA Sul decision of 10 May 2018, case 774/07.8BELRA](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtca.nsf/-/F1DB791331D4F3B68025828E005440BB), concerns an ordinary municipal assembly under earlier legislation and supports using dispatch rather than actual delivery for advance notice. It does not conclusively establish the endpoint rule for Arroios’ extraordinary sessions. Consequently, five/eight **full intervening calendar days** are a cautious planning baseline, not a newly asserted legal limit. The existing legal reference dates remain separate for convocation and edital, and missing only this baseline produces an internal planning warning. The user’s 0–5 working-day margins apply after this baseline. The extraordinary ten-day opening and the separate initiative rule remain unchanged. PAOD, activity information and individual-request rules are not reinterpreted by this change.

No assumed CTT transit day is added to formal notice. [CTT’s registered-mail service](https://www.ctt.pt/particulares/enviar/para-portugal/correio-registado) describes about one working day on the mainland as indicative. Receipt stays a separate, explicitly confirmed step. The 17:00 Lisbon cutoff remains an internal planning assumption; it is not a nationwide branch-hours claim or legal deadline.
